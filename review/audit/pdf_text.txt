01 — Decisions, scope and use of this document
1.1 Resolved brief
This blueprint retains the approved premise, mother–child relationship, living-bearer infrastructure, 
evolving Hold mechanic, controlled evacuation climax and final act of release. Leda is replaced throughout 
by Ivo, age 13. There is one canonical story and one ending.
The request contains incompatible duration targets: five to five-and-a-half hours in one passage and 
approximately “3.45 hours” at the end. The production baseline follows the final instruction and the 
previously approved concept: 3 hours 45 minutes, or 225 minutes. This is 3.75 decimal hours, not 3.45 
decimal hours. A five-hour campaign is not included. Expanding to 300–330 minutes would require a 
separately approved content and budget change, not slower walking or longer puzzle waits.
The 225-minute schedule includes first-time reasoning, ordinary exploration and modest retries; it 
excludes menus, pauses and end credits. Validate a median of 210–240 minutes with unfamiliar players. 
Experienced players may finish sooner; completion time cannot be guaranteed per individual. Do not add 
filler solely to reach a number.
1.2 What is locked and what must be tested
Locked creative decisions: Ivo; Anja; the coastal town; Hold's human cost; eight chapters; one personal 
Hold; no combat; no inventory crafting; no collectible-dependent ending; environmental explanation of 
essential facts; ending through prepared release.
Initial engineering/design values: movement speeds, jump geometry, Hold ranges, AI sight distances, room 
dimensions, camera damping, asset budgets and milestone weeks. These are explicit starting values, not 
measurements of INSIDE or proven production results. Changes require a recorded reason and regression 
tests.
Validation gates: movement quality, first-person understanding of Hold, response to the bearer revelation, 
reproducibility of puzzle physics, narrative comprehension, final network-control usability, campaign 
duration and minimum-spec performance. The team can begin preproduction immediately. Full asset 
production is gated on a successful vertical slice.
This package does not contain a playable engine project, finished concept paintings, final recordings or 
approved commercial rights. It specifies what those deliverables must be. No document can honestly 
certify unbuilt gameplay as already fun, bug-free or precisely timed.
1.3 Product envelope
Single-player, offline-first, side-on 2.5D psychological-horror puzzle-platformer. Launch baseline: Windows 
PC, controller and keyboard. Architect input, saves and rendering for later ports, but do not budget 
simultaneous console certification or mobile support into this baseline. Camera movement is authored; the 
player does not rotate it. Full 3D assets support constrained movement lanes, depth staging and lighting.
Target 60 frames per second at 1920×1080 on a provisional midrange PC test fixture. A 30 fps cap must 
remain mechanically valid. Exact supported hardware is set after benchmark testing, not copied from 
another game's store page. SDR is mandatory; HDR is outside baseline. No online accounts, multiplayer, 
user-generated content, procedural campaign or live-service economy.
The intended audience is adults who enjoy vulnerable protagonists, physical puzzles and unsettling 
narrative. A child protagonist is not a child-audience designation. Content includes confinement, implied 
bodily integration, drowning danger, capture and bereavement. Ratings and storefront disclosures require a 
later formal review.

1.4 Production ownership
The game director owns player experience and narrative consistency. The lead engineer owns system 
contracts and technical acceptance. Art and audio leads own the sensory benchmark. The producer owns 
scope, dependency tracking and gate decisions. QA owns reproducibility evidence, not responsibility for 
designing away every problem.
Each sequence has a stable ID, C01-S01 through C08-S06. Puzzle actors, checkpoints, narrative facts and 
test cases reference these IDs. IDs do not change when display names change. The master document 
describes intent; the production registers track assignments and progress. Both must change together 
when scope changes.

2.3 Hands-on reference task, week 1
The director, animator and gameplay engineer should capture a licensed local reference session. Examine

shared vocabulary for feel, not pixel-level imitation.

03 — Core vision and dramatic identity
3.1 Experience pillars
Useful control becomes intimate horror. Hold must genuinely help before its cost becomes clear. Its 
usefulness remains after the revelation; otherwise there is no conflict.
The environment is the story's physical argument. A staircase is not merely adjacent to a suffering bearer. 
The staircase's movement and the bearer's posture are demonstrably connected.
The body feels vulnerable; the controls remain trustworthy. Ivo stumbles, strains and breathes, but the 
game does not invent input delay or random failures to simulate fear.
Escalation changes meaning, not only spectacle. First a gate, then an unseen person, then an aware 
enemy, then Anja, then Ivo himself, then a district of people who depend on his decisions.
Release is earned through engineering. The ending is not a menu choice between “save” and “destroy.” 
The player spends the campaign replacing human burdens with safe physical alternatives.
3.2 Player fantasy and emotional arc
The initial fantasy is being small but resourceful: survive adult machinery using one strange tool. The 
midpoint replaces superiority with responsibility. The climax temporarily grants infrastructural control 
while removing bodily freedom. The resolution restores ordinary human scale and voluntary help.
Target feelings by act: curiosity and urgency in chapters 1–2; complicity and dread in 3–4; personal grief 
and threatened identity in 5–6; focused responsibility in 7; loss, relief and imperfect human connection in 
8.
There are no sanity points, hallucination combat arenas, collectible lore keys, morality totals, boss health 
bars, random jump scares or arbitrary monsters. No section should rely on a new exception to Hold's 
established rules.
3.3 The complete story
The town has lived inside an unfinished evacuation for years. During a catastrophic harbor surge, 
emergency machinery linked people to arrested structural and hydraulic processes. The original intention 
was temporary support while other workers repaired the damage and moved residents inland. Instead, 
departments normalized replacement bearers. Ordinary homes concealed permanent emergency 
connections.
Ivo knows the public fittings as safety equipment. His mother Anja maintains their external hardware. She 
knows they require attendants but believes central authorities rotate and relieve those attendants. She has 
repaired terminals, not chosen victims or understood central assimilation. This makes her involvement 
meaningful without turning her into a secretly omniscient villain.
During a new local collapse caused by deterioration of the old system, Anja holds their apartment landing 
so Ivo can cross. Custodians transfer her load to a transport rig and take her below. They are not rescuing 
her; the central bearer is failing, and the system needs a replacement. Ivo follows with a dropped portable 
brace.
He learns to stop machinery, leave its burden with anchors, and follow connections through domestic 
walls. He sees hints of people behind those fittings and eventually learns they remain aware. Mechanical 
supports can free some of them; he releases an elderly man under a staircase, whose significance is not 
explained.

At the passenger interchange, Ivo holds a Custodian's exposed harness. The man's eyes track him. The 
implication travels backward through everything the player has done. In the burden rooms, Ivo recognizes 
the mechanisms he used and the people who carried them.
He finds Anja already integrated into the central installation. Peripheral bearers have intact bodies; central 
bearers are physically distributed through structural tissues and conduits. Hold cannot repair damage or 
reverse time. Anja cannot simply be disconnected and restored. She recognizes her son, taps his hand 
twice, and refuses to let him occupy the adjoining replacement station.
Ivo follows the hydraulic loads to the seawall. Behind the suspended surge, the real sea is low. The original 
storm has passed. The trapped water and unstable buildings remain dangerous because the town never 
completed a controlled release. His goal changes from taking Anja home to ending the condition that will 
otherwise bind her—and him—indefinitely.
He clears discharge channels, engages mechanical supports and opens routes for intact survivors. The 
Custodians increasingly treat him as installation material rather than a fugitive. He takes the central 
junction temporarily, directing restrained machinery so survivors can move themselves to safety. With 
each load physically resolved, the network contracts.
Ivo withdraws before permanent integration. Anja remains at the root connection. He reaches her final 
recognizable hand, receives two taps, and takes the last Hold through the portable brace. Releasing the 
input starts the discharge along the route he prepared. The remaining central tissue relaxes; Anja dies. The 
game neither reverses this loss nor reveals it as a dream.
Ivo escapes the brief structural settling using ordinary movement. At a dead exit terminal, the elderly 
bearer he freed holds a gate open with his own hands. The gesture is voluntary and temporary. On the 
inland embankment, Ivo gradually uncurls his own hand and accepts another survivor's hand. The footsteps 
do not synchronize. The screen fades only after control has returned for the final steps.
3.4 Fiction boundaries that prevent contradictions
The device arrests a connected physical process, not time. Awareness, unrelated dust, rainfall, eyes and 
breathing continue. A held staircase does not freeze the room. The origin of the technology remains 
unspecified, but its observable operation is consistent.
Central integration is a physical procedure involving a root collar and distributed contacts, not a random 
punishment for using Hold too often. Ivo's growing tremor and postural mimicry are effects of carrying 
linked strain. His reversible trial connection in chapter 6 foreshadows the central junction's controls. 
Permanent integration requires the closing installation apparatus, which he prevents by unloading the 
network.
The town does not secretly read Ivo's intentions. Its equipment responds to load and continuity; 
Custodians interpret his actions. They can misjudge him, but they do not teleport or know his location 
through walls without an authored sensor path.
Freeing every historical victim is not possible. The story's surviving cohort consists of intact bearers and 
remaining residents along the active route. Do not stage the ending as a mathematically perfect evacuation 
of an unseen entire city. The camera establishes who reaches safety, while distant damage remains outside 
the player's controllable scope.
3.5 Character and threat bible
CHR-IVO — Ivo, 13. Approximately 1.52 m in the production scale; slight but not emaciated. Ash-blue 
short jacket, pale ribbed undershirt visible at the collar, dark cropped trousers, worn lace-up shoes. Uneven 
hair, no glowing eyes, no red-shirt silhouette. Right-handed brace; left hand performs the familiar two-tap 
response. A child who has helped with repairs: he observes, tests, recoils and persists. He never performs 
an adult weightlifter's pull or an acrobatic double jump. Four additive strain stages change shoulders, 
fingers and breath without changing movement timing.

CHR-ANJA — Anja, mother and maintenance worker. Work trousers, rolled sleeve, short practical hair, a 
double-stitched cuff that remains identifiable after integration. Three presentations use one underlying 
human identity: normal, transport harness, integrated hand/face/structural proxies. Recognition is through 
gesture and cuff, not spoken exposition. The central reveal must not require graphic close-up mutilation.
CHR-ORREN — Orren, the elderly bearer. A former repair worker, recognizable by a missing coat button 
and one bent shoe. Freed in C03-S04, glimpsed recovering in C05-S01, seen crossing in C07-S04, and 
holding the exit in C08-S05. Never joins as a commanded companion; no escort AI over the entire 
campaign.
CHR-CUSTODIAN — Custodian, two variants. A constrained human worker inside a padded emergency 
harness and waterproof coat. The covered variant's live port is physically shielded, not magically immune. 
The exposed variant has a white ceramic contact visible on the chest or forearm. Faces are tired and 
watchful, not blank masks copied from another game. The threat is capture and forced placement. Use two 
model variants on one skeleton and one behavioral framework.
CHR-PORTER — The Braced Porter. A human rescue worker suspended from a counterweighted overhead 
apparatus. Long hoses and equipment—not elongated anatomy—create the silhouette. The rig repeatedly 
reaches, secures and retracts. Eyes and small breath motions continue when the main harness is held. One 
main encounter and one later distant recognition shot; no repeated miniboss fights.
CHR-BEARER — Intact bearer family. Four base body/clothing variants, six constrained pose sets, limited 
facial and hand motion. Reuse combined variants for the survivor cohort. They are people, not an enemy 
species. Important individuals need a distinctive prop or posture, not dialogue trees.
ENV-ROOT — Root installation. The apparent “creature” is architecture bearing a person's breathing 
rhythm: tense surfaces, hand-shaped fittings, structural bands and a partially visible human face. It has no 
separate monster personality and never becomes a combat boss.

04 — Player systems and the evolving Hold mechanic
4.1 Core loop
Enter a readable composition; recognize a route and a physical obstruction; inspect the movement or 
connection; manipulate or Hold; observe who or what carries the strain; create a safe route or physical 
replacement; traverse; encounter a consequence that changes the meaning of the next use.
The short loop is approximately 30–120 seconds. A major puzzle may contain several loops and take 3–7 
minutes on first encounter. Avoid two consecutive puzzles with the same reasoning structure and 
decorative changes only. “Stop at the correct angle” can teach the tool; it cannot be the entire campaign.

The ballistic values follow the proposed velocity and gravity, not reference-game measurements. Validate 
at 30, 60, 120 and uncapped rendering with a 60 Hz gameplay simulation. Falling farther than 3.2 m is 
lethal unless the landing volume explicitly supports a designed slide or catch. Mark lethal geometry by

Ivo can move, crouch, jump and use a short one-handed ledge catch while maintaining a personal Hold. A 
two-handed ladder climb or heavy push cannot start while his brace hand is occupied. The rejected action 
points his gaze to the brace and nearby anchor; it does not silently release the load. This restriction must 
be demonstrated in C02-S02 before it becomes dangerous.
No swimming system is included. Shallow water slows movement to 2.4 m/s and changes sound. Deep-
water boundaries are visible, have a recoverable shallow warning shelf where possible, and use short 
drowning failure animations, not a hidden oxygen puzzle.
4.4 Target and connection rules
A portable Hold starts on a compatible exposed contact within 3.2 m, in the facing half-plane, and with 
unobstructed acquisition visibility. Once acquired, it persists within a 6 m connection radius; ordinary 
occlusion does not cancel an established electrical link. The brace's ceramic vane begins bending at 5 m 
and clicks at 5.7 m. A 0.4-second break warning prevents a single-frame boundary error. Returning within 
range during that warning preserves the Hold.
A conduit contact changes the source of reach, not the target rule. The player stands at or connects to a 
visible branch, and an eligible endpoint can be addressed through the authored graph. Electrical distance is 
not inferred from decorative pipes. Interactive conduit strips use a distinct ceramic seam and physical 
junction marks. The late junction has a curated visible set of nodes; it does not expose an unreadable world 
graph.
Candidate selection scores facing, distance and author-assigned priority, with hysteresis so the target does 
not flicker between nearby ports. One unambiguous animation cue shows the current candidate: Ivo 
extends the brace fingers toward it. Optional accessibility outlining supplements, rather than replaces, that 
cue. An active Hold never automatically switches because another object becomes closer.

4.5 The Hold state machine
A process can be Running, HeldPersonal, HeldAnchor, Supported, Disconnected or Resolved. The last 
three are not synonyms. Supported means ordinary structure carries the load; disconnected means the 
device cannot address it; resolved means the authored narrative dependency has been safely removed.
Acquiring Hold captures the process coordinate, its commanded velocity, mode and owner. While held, 
coordinate progression is zero. The system does not bank elapsed motor work or accumulated force. On 
release, a motor resumes its captured phase and nominal velocity; a gravity process resumes its captured 
velocity and subsequent acceleration. It does not replay missed simulation steps. Already-broken objects 
stay broken.
This is a fictional local restraint, not a physically conservative time simulation. Presentation must 
communicate the same rule every time. No arbitrary delayed explosions are caused simply by holding 
longer.
Only one owner sustains a process or engineered assembly at once. Transfer is atomic: validate 
destination, assign the new owner at a fixed simulation boundary, release the prior owner, then publish 
feedback. A transfer failure leaves the original Hold intact. No frame may exist with neither owner or two 
independently controlling owners.
4.6 Transfer and relief
An anchor contains an attached bearer and one load socket. “Free anchor” means its socket is not currently 
carrying a load; it does not mean no person is inside. An occupied anchor cannot accept a second personal 
process. Existing loads can be taken over by Ivo only when the interface and authored capacity permit it.
With a personal Hold active, approaching a free compatible anchor and pressing Interact transfers that 
Hold. Once transfer completes, releasing Hold does not release the anchor. Taking the load back requires a 
deliberate interaction with that same fitting. This prevents the common ambiguity in which letting go of a 
trigger unexpectedly drops an externally supported platform.
Relief is a physical condition. A ratchet engages, a prop reaches a support socket, a channel drains, or a 
structure settles into a verified stable state. The process then releases its owner without moving 
dangerously. The bearer can relax. A support graphic alone is insufficient; the puzzle actor must validate 
contact, orientation and lock state.
4.7 Capacity and progression
Portable contacts accept one local process. Assembly stations combine a predefined set of coupled 
restraints and require a braced stationary posture. The central junction aggregates a defined evacuation 
subsystem. Capacity is determined by interface type, not invisible weight statistics or a growing magic-
energy pool.
Chapters 1–2 teach arrest and transfer. Chapter 3 exposes conduit reach and ordinary support. Chapter 4 
exposes a human harness previously covered by protective equipment. Chapter 5 teaches dependency 
relief. Chapter 6 teaches stationary assembly selection in a safe three-node rehearsal. Chapter 7 applies 
the same controls to a larger, visible network. Chapter 8 makes deliberate release the culminating action.
The player is not punished with permanent corruption for optional experimentation. Four visual strain 
stages change at authored integration events, not after an arbitrary number of button presses. Their 
release and persistence are narrative state, saved independently of health or retries.
4.8 Failure and survival
No combat damage economy: capture, crush, major fall and deep-water failure reset the current encounter. 
Deaths should be abrupt and distressing, not prolonged spectacles involving a child. Use approximately

5.2 Shared sequence contract
Each sequence must have an entry state, reachable forward goal, visible cause-and-effect, reversible 
exploration state where practical, and a validated exit snapshot. Global fiction is not explained by invisible 
trigger text. All checkpoint IDs below identify required checkpoint opportunities; the final implementation 
may merge adjacent safe snapshots only after replay-time tests pass. A narrative commit can be stored 
without repositioning the player.
Chapter 01 — The Evacuation
Campaign interval: 0:00–0:20. Target: 20 minutes. Arrest motion; lose the mother; establish trustworthy 
control.
C01-S01 — A hand on the table (3 min)
Type and blockout: Playable opening. 18 m apartment-to-landing route; one depth connector.
What the player sees: A dim kitchen. A spoon rattles in a cup although the room looks still. Anja repairs a 
porcelain wall fitting. Ivo sits at the table; the camera is already in gameplay position. At three seconds, 
directional input lets him stand and move. His pale collar separates him from the dark dresser.
Player action and solution: At 00:03–00:35, walk to Anja. She offers two taps when close, not on a fixed 
timer. At 00:35–01:10, Interact slides a light chair 0.8 m out of the doorway: an unthreatened grip lesson. 
At 01:10–02:00, follow her through the hall and step a 0.2 m threshold. At 02:00–03:00, she checks the 
landing terminal as the public alarm changes. Optional inspection of the cup adds seconds, not a collectible.
Threat, horror and fairness: No lethal threat. Dust falls while the hand fitting remains motionless. Anja 
waits naturally at the door if Ivo explores. The alarm builds urgency without forcibly moving him.

06 — Puzzle construction and horror direction
6.1 Puzzle grammar and progression
Use six reasoning families: timing/orientation, allocation, connection, physical support, behavior-after-
release and dependency resolution. Each puzzle combines an obvious immediate goal with a less obvious 
arrangement of available means. Later puzzles should add a relationship, not merely another lever.
P01–P03 establish local motion and safe traversal. P04–P07 add ownership and external support. P08–
P11 add reachable networks, geometry and durable solutions. P12–P15 connect awareness, enemy 
behavior and physical obstruction. P16–P19 require ordered relief and reinterpret Ivo as prospective 
infrastructure. P20 rehearses the final interface. P21–P23 prepare the three release prerequisites. P24–
P26 apply those lessons to people and withdrawal. P27–P29 verify, release and escape.
Twenty-nine named puzzle/interaction challenges are distributed across 48 sequences. Not all are equal-
sized puzzle rooms. A dramatic recognition beat must not be padded with a token lever merely to count as 
gameplay.
6.2 Implementation example: P10 staircase relief
Author the staircase as a gravity-driven hinge with a bounded travel coordinate q, a service catch at the 
lower limit, a ratchet acceptance band and a support state. Ivo does not need to find a mathematically 
exact angle. Initial ratchet band: 32–38 degrees, with a broad visible tooth; stair traversal is valid 
throughout that band.
Entry state: Orren owns the staircase load; ratchet disengaged; access recess open; Ivo owns no personal 
Hold. Ivo acquires through the service port. Transfer commits ownership to Ivo, leaving Orren attached but 
unloaded. Interact on the adjacent lever requests ratchet engagement only inside the acceptance band. 
Outside the band, the lever physically stops against the next tooth and can be retried.
On release, a valid ratchet takes the load and marks Supported. The bearer-release animation begins only 
when the support is committed. Orren moves to a navigation refuge before the next checkpoint. Invalid 
release settles toward the service catch; Orren is not killed as a hidden moral punishment. A reset restores 
the current reasoning milestone, not the whole chapter.
Required alternate solutions: any permitted angle inside the band; either keyboard or controller; personal 
Hold held or toggled. Rejected exploits: freezing Orren’s hand rather than the staircase port; using 
incidental debris as a support; transferring into an occupied anchor; cancelling the handover animation to 
duplicate a load owner. Tests include holding for ten minutes, rapid recapture and quit/reload between 
support commitment and Orren’s first step.
6.3 Implementation example: P13 one hand, two dangers
States: SafePreparation → TrolleyStaged → WorkerAware → WorkerHeld → TrolleyReleased → 
WorkerBlocked → ShutterHeld → Escaped. State names organize diagnostics, but a valid physical 
alternative is not rejected just because events arrive in a different order.
The trolley uses a one-dimensional rail and a gravity process with a hard stop across the worker lane. Ivo 
pushes it before maintaining a personal Hold. Brake release is one-handed and remains valid while his right 
hand carries the worker’s harness. The worker’s blocked response is a known heaving action, initially 4.5 
seconds. The shutter contact is reachable from the protected side during that window. Tune to leave at 
least 0.75 seconds of margin after a correctly executed crossing.
The scene must provide a sheltered reset crank so early trolley release cannot softlock it. The worker never 
changes speed secretly to rescue a bad solution or invalidate a good one. Assist mode extends the heaving 
window and detection windup; it does not change the logical answer.

6.4 Implementation example: P24–P25 network finale
Station input differs visibly from portable grip because the station contains mechanical latches: press Hold 
to arrest/latch the selected running branch; press Interact to release the selected held branch; directional 
input selects an adjacent visible node. Releasing the physical Hold button alone does not unlatch a station 
branch. This is first demonstrated in C06-S02, with the same raised/lowered lever states used at anchors. 
On disconnect, portable hold/release behavior returns. Optional local glyphs communicate the current 
context.
The station can maintain a predefined aggregate assembly. It is not unrestricted telekinesis. Selection is 
navigation, not ownership transfer. Branches retain their state while the camera moves. The two 
evacuation puzzle views expose at most three candidates each, with predictable left/right adjacency and a 
return-to-body action through the existing Interact prompt at the body node.
Final dependency chain: prepared tram supports → tram settlement → first group crossing → first shutter 
separator → supported canopy and aligned landing → second group crossing → last separator → 
withdrawal condition. Each arrow corresponds to a visible mechanical condition. NPCs enter a path only 
when its currently required supports are safe. They do not run knowingly beneath a descending roof solely 
to manufacture tragedy.
The climax can be failed through a clearly telegraphed local capture or through Ivo’s later physical escape, 
but experimenting with a branch does not secretly erase survivors elsewhere. Safety stops make incorrect 
release attempts observable and recoverable. This preserves dread without introducing a punitive strategy-
game layer at the end.
6.5 Horror director rules
Tension has five authored states: Quiet, Uneasy, Investigating, Pursuit and Aftermath. These states drive 
audio density, breathing, background animation and camera framing. They never grant enemies 
information, modify puzzle answers, consume a sanity meter or spawn a random threat behind the player.
The major horror escalations are specific: Anja cannot leave the landing; an anchor inhales; a fingernail 
moves; a wall holds a kitchen; a frozen worker remains aware; the familiar machines map to people; Anja is 
physically distributed; the sea has already receded; Ivo’s breathing moves a corridor; the player loses bodily 
locomotion; releasing the root ends Anja’s life.
Use approximately 45–120 seconds of reduced immediate threat after a major pursuit. A quiet section still 
contains meaningful observation, traversal or a new spatial relationship. No required puzzle should be 
interrupted by an unseen enemy before the player has had a safe opportunity to inspect its rules.
Do not use extreme volume spikes, full-screen flashing, random camera seizure, fake application errors or 
unreliable controls. The player’s body can look distressed without the player’s input becoming 
untrustworthy. Intensity settings may reduce startle layers, shake and visual distress without concealing 
essential narrative facts.
6.6 Environmental storytelling checklist
Every essential discovery appears at least twice through different channels: motion plus body response; 
visible connection plus repeated sound; physical support plus bearer release. Optional props enrich 
interpretation but never carry the sole explanation for Anja’s fate or the release prerequisites.
A story prop must answer one of four questions: who lived here, what did they believe the fittings did, 
what has been hidden, or what changed because of Ivo. Avoid generalized dirt and unrelated surgical 
clutter. A neatly repaired anchor hidden under new wallpaper is more specific than another blood stain.
For each room, the narrative designer writes a one-sentence intended inference and one plausible 
mistaken inference. A test fails when players consistently form the mistaken inference that blocks the next 
objective. Mystery about origin is acceptable; confusion about usable rules is not.

07 — Enemy, creature and survivor systems
7.1 Custodian behavior contract
Use an explicit finite-state machine: Patrol, Inspect, Suspicious, SearchLastKnown, Pursue, Blocked, 
CaptureWindup, Capture, Held and Recover. Covered/exposed is an equipment state, not a separate 
intelligence model. A worker can only enter Held through an exposed compatible port.
Initial tuning: patrol 1.2 m/s; investigation 2.1 m/s; pursuit 4.5 m/s; capture reach 0.65 m; capture windup 
0.65 s. The first chase adds a 2 s initial recognition pause. Later chases can shorten recognition only after 
their threat has been safely introduced. These numbers are tunable and must be verified against the actual 
route and Ivo’s 4.2 m/s maximum.
Sight uses a facing sector, distance falloff, line-of-sight samples at chest/head, and authored exposure 
volumes. Initial forward range is 9 m; proximity recognition applies within 1.5 m in clear view. A suspicion 
accumulator rises with visible exposure, rather than instant capture on one ray. Initial full-exposure 
recognition is 0.9 s; decay after lost sight is 0.35 per second. Background-only workers use explicit staging 
roles and cannot become foreground pursuers accidentally.
Hearing uses gameplay noise events with a position and category, not raw audio volume. Initial radii: slow 
cloth movement 2 m, running on metal 7 m, trolley impact 10 m. A wall or closed service door applies 
authored attenuation. Hearing supplies a last-known investigation point, not perfect tracking. Caption or 
visual response makes important noise consequences accessible.
Search lasts approximately 6–10 s, visits at most two nearby authored points, then returns to the route 
unless new evidence arrives. The AI cannot see through an opaque blanket because the lighting happens to 
be bright. Thin wet sheets are not valid opaque cover; their visual and sensor treatment share the same 
CoverDefinition.
7.2 Capture, restraint and recovery
Capture requires an active CaptureWindup, line-of-contact and overlap with the correct gameplay lane. 
Disable capture immediately when the harness is held. Preserve last-known position, target intent and path 
state while held, but pause locomotion and the associated capture timer. Eye tracking and breathing 
remain presentation-only.
On release, play a short physical recovery—initially 0.35 s—then resume the appropriate behavior. Do not 
reset awareness to Patrol merely because the player froze the worker. This makes post-release planning 
meaningful. A physical obstruction uses Blocked with a known interaction or alternate path, not phasing 
through props.
The capture result moves Ivo into a short paired restraint animation using a validated alignment point. If 
alignment is invalid, fall back to a safe generic caught pose rather than pulling him through scenery. 
Death/capture loops should not linger graphically on harm to a child.
7.3 Braced Porter contract
The Porter is a motion-path actor, not a free-roaming NavMesh monster. States: Traverse, ReachWindup, 
Secure, Retract, InspectBasket, Held and Resume. Initial cycle is about 9–12 s depending on rail section, 
with a minimum 0.8 s visible ReachWindup. A demonstration cycle plays before the player enters its 
capture envelope.
The single main restraint process drives carriage position and harness pose as one authored assembly. 
Holding it stops that assembly and disables capture. Loose hoses, clothing and small facial movements 
continue. The suspended basket is a registered moving platform with a stable support transform, not an 
unstable free-swinging Rigidbody essential to progression.

7.4 Survivor behavior
Use route graphs with refuge nodes, occupancy checks and a small set of contextual actions: wait, stand, 
walk carefully, help another person, step over a threshold, clear a gate and rest. No crowd simulation is 
required. Maximum visible moving cohort target: 12 individuals; maximum simultaneously active detailed 
agents: 8, with lower-detail route proxies for the rest.
Survivors enter a crossing only after its support predicate passes. Once committed, moving gates remain 
interlocked until the whole group clears. A recovery timeout returns a blocked actor to the nearest 
already-reached refuge using a hidden offscreen correction only when necessary; visible teleportation is a 
defect. Prefer correcting navigation geometry before relying on recovery logic.
Each survivor has a stable ID, current refuge, route phase and presentation variant. The final count is the 
established cohort count, not a speculative population statistic. Orren’s four appearances use the same 
saved narrative identity and distinctive assets.

08 — Art direction, cinematography and animation
8.1 Overall image
A restrained, painterly-industrial 3D world seen from a controlled side-on camera. Forms should read as 
large, simple masses; small detail is concentrated where hands, feet, light and story make contact. The 
signature is not monochrome imitation. It is domestic architecture connected by white ceramic hand 
fittings, with weight and respiration passing through buildings.
Color anchors: charcoal #20272A, damp green-gray #586562, porcelain #D4D0C4, oxidized brass 
#7B7460 and weak amber #B69768. These are art-direction reference swatches, not physically meaningful 
lighting values. Use color-managed textures and verify the final graded result on calibrated and ordinary 
displays. A light object is not automatically interactable; material, shape and animation jointly communicate 
affordance.
Large value groups should dominate: dark foreground frame, readable midground play lane, softened 
background story. Keep enough local separation around Ivo to identify his feet and hands during danger. 
Darkness must not erase the platform edge that determines survival.
8.2 Architecture and environment progression
Chapters 1–3 use modest apartments, tiled communal rooms, timber repairs, cast-iron rails and masonry 
cavities. Human-scale repairs are the distinguishing detail: patched wallpaper, mismatched screws, tied 
sleeves, a support concealed behind a cupboard. Avoid ornate Gothic architecture and generic science-
fiction laboratories.
Chapter 4 expands into a civic tram hall: glazed partitions, broad roof trusses, ticket cabinets, luggage lanes 
and emergency transfer equipment. Chapter 5 exposes the backs of those same components through 
cramped maintenance rooms. Chapter 6 opens into monumental hydraulic galleries, counterweights, 
seawall ribs and arrested water. Chapter 7 revisits familiar infrastructure from new connected views; it is 
not a brand-new biome. Chapter 8 resolves into bare stone, daylight and an ordinary embankment.
Scale grammar: doors usually 2.0–2.2 m; domestic ceilings 2.5–3.1 m; industrial bays 5–12 m; major 
hydraulic views 20–40 m high. Larger structures emphasize Ivo’s scale, but playable props retain reachable 
controls and coherent human maintenance access.
8.3 Materials and surface detail
Build a small master-material family: painted plaster, wet plaster, timber, oxidized metal, painted metal, 
porcelain, fabric, skin, glass, water and structural membrane. Use restrained base-color variation and 
readable roughness differences. Do not depend on noisy scanned surfaces to create atmosphere.
Porcelain has rounded worn edges, hairline cracks and occasional exposed skin at narrative locations. The 
normal intact version must not visibly reveal the twist from every room. Structural membranes resemble 
stressed building material with human tension, not a glossy extraterrestrial organism. Keep obvious 
wetness localized to hydraulic areas and contact points.
Default texel-density planning: approximately 256 px/m on ordinary environment surfaces, 512 px/m on 
hero interaction surfaces; hero close-up hands can use dedicated texture allocation. Validate actual on-
screen needs before increasing resolution. Most ordinary textures target 1K–2K; 4K is reserved for 
justified hero assets or shared atlases. Decals carry repair history and localized lighting treatment without 
flooding every surface with unique textures.

8.4 Lighting and atmosphere
Use a linear-color workflow with restrained tone mapping and fixed or slowly authored exposure per 
scene. Avoid automatic exposure pumping when a bright doorway enters frame. The lighting baseline is 
baked/static environmental contribution where useful, light probes or an equivalent character solution, a 
few selected real-time key lights, and art-directed fills/cookies/decals. This is a proposed modern 
implementation, not a claim that INSIDE used this exact stack. [R02]
A puzzle’s critical moving parts must remain readable through their full travel. Put the informative surface 
on the lit side of the object; do not rely on an invisible highlight revealed only at one camera angle. A held 
state is shown by stopped motion, a strained vane and a changed sound—not neon magic particles.
Fog separates depth, not foreground interaction. Use bounded low-resolution volumetric or mesh-based 
fog appropriate to the chosen render path. Do not make a custom volumetric renderer a prerequisite for 
the prototype. Rain is mostly near-camera streaks, surface wetness and carefully placed splashes. No 
essential route becomes unreadable because weather density changed randomly.

8.7 Animation direction
Locomotion authority stays with the controller. Animation supplies anticipation, contact and recovery. Use 
in-place locomotion with velocity-driven blends; use bounded root-motion alignment only for authored 
interactions, captures and short paired gestures. Do not wait for a decorative animation to finish before 
honoring a valid jump or stop.
Layer foot placement and hand contact over authored clips. Foot IK operates within small correction limits 
and turns off appropriately during jumps or steep falls. Hands reach for a rail before grip, settle at a valid 
socket and release without popping. The brace hand keeps a consistent contact pose throughout a Hold; 
force expression is an additive shoulder/finger layer.
A four-stage strain set includes transient wrist echo, persistent finger curl, shoulder alignment with a large 
support, and stationary full-body bracing. These are narrative presentation states, not stamina penalties. 
After withdrawal, reverse the large-scale tension while retaining the curled hand until the epilogue.
Death presentation combines short authored failures with controlled ragdoll follow-through only where it 
remains readable. Avoid ragdoll chaos, exploding limbs or repeated graphic spectacle. The Porter uses 
authored rail motion plus secondary hoses and a constrained body rig. Anja’s integrated proxies share one 
breathing phase and identity; they are not independently acting clones.

moving-platform shadows, TAA trails, facial readability during Hold and puzzle endpoints at the darkest

09 — Audio, music and tactile feedback
9.1 Audio identity
The town sounds like machinery borrowing the body. A motor’s interrupted rhythm reappears as breath, 
tendon-like creak or a minute knock behind a wall. Keep exact callback relationships for the laundry, stair, 
tram and Anja; do not layer random groans over every machine.
Primary sound families: room air and distant weather; footsteps and fabric; machine cycles; restraint 
engagement; bearer strain; human gestures; threat movement; water pressure and release. Music is sparse 
and textural, emerging from these sources rather than accompanying every room with a continuous 
melody.
9.2 Dynamic parameters and timing
Use parameters HoldActive, ProcessPhase, StrainStage, ThreatState, InteriorExterior, NetworkLoadBand 
and RootReleased. ProcessPhase comes from gameplay authority. The audio engine schedules or 
crossfades to that state; it cannot independently decide when a lethal gate closes.
Audio can request a noncritical presentation beat at the next compatible phase—such as aligning a 
breathing animation or delaying a safe background knock. The game validates the request. Dangerous 
transitions never depend on sound-device latency or a missed callback. This implements a bounded form of 
the two-way relationship discussed in Playdead’s audio material. [R04]
On Hold acquisition, the machine’s moving component stops while room resonance decays. A bodily strain 
layer enters at the corresponding rate. On transfer, move the apparent source toward the anchor, with an 
unmistakable contact click. On relief, remove tension rather than playing a reward jingle. On final release, 
ordinary moving water replaces the sustained pressure tone.
9.3 Spatial mix and silence
Treat near gameplay cues, medium-distance room machinery and distant story sounds as separate 
priorities. Dialogue is not used to explain objectives. Breath recordings must be varied enough to avoid an 
obvious repeating panic loop during a long puzzle.
Silence means reduced density, not digital muting. In the fingernail reveal, preserve room air and one small 
ceramic sound. After the junction, remove the shared rhythmic bed so irregular footsteps become 
meaningful. Use a dynamic-range option for headphones, speakers and low-volume play; essential 
warnings must survive each mix.
Occlusion is tied to portal and material states, not repeated expensive raycasts for every voice. A closed 
service door muffles sound; an opened hatch restores its high frequencies. A held worker’s breathing 
remains localized to his body even when his main motion layer stops.
9.4 Music and recording brief
Commission a small original palette: bowed metal, breath-shaped textures, low strings or processed 
resonances, restrained piano or struck ceramic fragments. Avoid imitating recognizable motifs, samples or 
signature processing chains from the references. Compose stems for uneasy exploration, first pursuit, 
bearer recognition, flood revelation, central junction and final release; several scenes should have no 
scored layer.
Record Ivo through age-appropriate performance direction and appropriate production safeguards. His 
sound is effort, fear and fatigue, not constant exaggerated gasping. Anja needs breaths, small exertions and 
the two-tap motif. Custodians need restrained calls without intelligible exposition, clothing and harness 
movement. No essential word must be decoded from distorted speech.

10.4 Character controller
Implement a capsule-sweep kinematic motor constrained to a lane. It owns velocity, ground contacts, jump 
buffering, slope handling, moving-platform support and authored traversal transitions. Query static and 
registered moving geometry each simulation tick. Do not let animation root motion and a dynamic 
Rigidbody both drive the same character.
The movement state machine is Grounded, Airborne, Crouched, GripMove, LedgeHang, Climb, 
StationConnected, Captured and Dead. Each transition defines allowed inputs, collision shape, animation 
request and snapshot fields. Movement modifiers are explicit: shallow water, push rail and authored slide. 
Strain animation cannot silently modify speed.
Moving platforms expose previous/current support transforms. The motor applies their point displacement 
before local movement and preserves an appropriate bounded departure velocity on jump. Use swept 
collision for moving hazards and fast platform edges. The player must not penetrate a held platform when 
it changes ownership.
A crush detector compares conflicting support/contact surfaces and their approach direction, with a small 
tolerance. It is not “any two contacts equals death.” Test corners, low ceilings, rotating platforms, slope 
transitions and door thresholds. Incidental debris does not crush the player; only registered lethal 
mechanisms do.

10.5 Simulation and physics
Use a 60 Hz fixed gameplay tick as the initial baseline, independent of rendering. Read and timestamp 
input in the frame loop, consume commands once at fixed boundaries, then interpolate presentation. 
Unity’s documentation distinguishes fixed updates from rendered frames and explains Rigidbody 
interpolation; the project still needs its own controller/platform integration tests. [R09]
Tick order: consume commands → validate Hold/transfer requests → advance authored processes → 
apply platform motion and motor collision → evaluate hazards and AI transitions → evaluate 
support/puzzle predicates → commit typed events and snapshots. Presentation observes the resulting 
state. Profile catch-up behavior; do not solve poor frame pacing by running an unbounded number of 
simulation steps.
Tier A, puzzle-authoritative motion: sliders, hinges, rotators, winches, tracked carts, counterweights, 
harnesses and water gates use scalar coordinates or bounded assemblies. Motors use fixed motion curves 
or speed profiles. Gravity mechanisms use a controlled integrator with explicit limits and catches. Their 
state can be serialized and restored without reconstructing a chaotic simulation.
Tier B, contact presentation: hand/foot IK, rope guides, cloth response, short ragdoll follow-through and 
spring dampers add weight. They cannot decide whether a puzzle is solved.
Tier C, incidental simulation: small debris, loose fabric and secondary objects can use ordinary 
rigidbody/cloth behavior or baked animation. They are culled, pooled and reset locally. They never become 
mandatory improvised supports unless explicitly promoted to Tier A.
For a held process, capture q and qVelocity. Freeze advancement, not the entire GameObject hierarchy. 
On release restore the permitted captured velocity, then normal drive/gravity; no accumulated force 
backlog. Before moving, sweep the relevant collider and resolve obstruction through the process’s policy: 
stop, crush after a telegraphed threshold, or stall. Policies are authored and visible through 
material/mechanism behavior.
Do not claim cross-machine bitwise determinism for a physics engine. The requirement is reproducible 
authored outcomes and semantically equivalent saves, verified under supported frame rates and hardware.

10.7 Interaction and puzzle framework
Every interactable exposes one or more InteractionSocket records: position, facing, approach lane, hand 
requirements, action type, eligible states and safe alignment envelope. Candidate selection uses stable 
priority and hysteresis. Interactions do not globally lock movement unless the action actually requires it.
A PuzzleDefinition references actors, entry snapshot, completion predicates, reversible milestones, 
checkpoint proposals and diagnostic labels. Avoid an enormous generic “solve any puzzle” graph editor. 
Build reusable components for HoldableProcess, SupportSocket, RailMover, Ratchet, CoverVolume, 
RouteGate, ConduitSwitch and ScenarioStep, then compose specific room logic in small readable 
orchestrators.
Scenario steps are idempotent: Enter, Active, Completed, Skipped and Restored. A callback may be 
received more than once without duplicating an NPC, opening an already removed gate again or replaying 
a one-shot reveal. Exit conditions query authoritative state, not whether a cinematic happened to reach its 
last frame.
Each puzzle has a developer reset button, a known solved snapshot, a dependency readout and a list of 
reachable reversible states. Debug overlays show q, owner, support, path validity and checkpoint safety. 
These tools are mandatory by the first complete chapter.
10.8 Saves and checkpoints
Store versioned semantic snapshots containing chapter/chunk IDs, player lane and pose, velocity and 
locomotion state, active Hold and owner graph, process coordinates, support/resolution flags, AI 
awareness/route phase, survivor refuges, narrative facts and cinematic step. Decorative physics can 
respawn from authored seeds or settled presets. Never save raw scene instance IDs as persistent identity.
Autosave after stable puzzle milestones and safe encounter entry/exit. Target replay loss during danger of 
roughly 20–45 seconds; exceptionally longer intervals require playtest approval. The required checkpoint 
opportunities are listed per sequence. Treat death recovery and durable disk saving separately: an in-
memory encounter snapshot enables fast restart, while durable writes occur asynchronously after a 
coherent state capture.
Save ordering: capture immutable state on the gameplay thread → serialize away from time-critical work 
→ write temporary file → flush/close → atomically replace the current save where supported → retain one 
previous valid backup. Include a checksum for corruption detection and a schema version for migration; 
the checksum is not a security system. On failure, preserve the last valid save and display a discreet but 
readable notification at a safe moment.
Restore ordering: load manifest and required chunks → register stable IDs → apply process/support states 
→ resolve owners and conduit links → place AI/survivors → restore player → restore narrative/cinematic 
stage → synchronize presentation → enable input. Hold inputs resume in a protected state until a fresh 
deliberate command; device reconnect or focus restoration must not drop the final root.
Suspend-on-quit captures the current semantic interaction. For a noninterruptible paired animation, finish 
or reconstruct its nearest defined safe substate, with no loss beyond the current short interaction. State 
this behavior accurately; do not promise a byte-exact reconstruction of arbitrary ragdoll poses. An OS 
crash falls back to the latest completed durable checkpoint.
Chapter replay is unlocked only for reached chapters and uses authored entry presets in a separate replay 
session. It must not overwrite a later main-campaign save accidentally. Provide reset-progress 
confirmation. Cloud sync is optional and requires conflict handling if integrated; it is not a launch 
dependency.

10.9 Streaming and loading
Plan approximately 16–24 streaming chunks, each containing two or three adjacent sequences where 
possible. Keep current, next and the nearest return chunk resident; adjust by measured memory. Load 
dependencies early using level manifests and retain shared materials/rigs without duplicating bundles.
A live personal Hold pins its target chunk until released or safely handed to a resident proxy. Cross-chunk 
mechanisms require explicit proxy ownership and a handoff test. Do not unload an anchor simply because 
it is behind the camera while its load is still relevant. Avoid long-distance active physics across the 
campaign; later callback views use saved state proxies.
Preload and activate scenes in controlled slices with gameplay actors initially dormant until their 
restoration stage. Unity’s Addressables documentation warns that withholding scene activation can block 
the asynchronous operation queue; do not park numerous inactive scene loads and assume other asset 
loads continue normally. [R10]
No loading screens between ordinary chapters. Use occluded corridors, doors, lifts and camera framing to 
provide scheduling headroom, but do not force unnecessary slow walks just to hide a broken loader. Initial 
boot and explicit chapter replay may show a simple loading indicator. Critical shaders, short audio cues and 
animation data are warmed before their first dangerous use.
10.10 Animation, camera and cinematic integration
Animation receives motor speed, contact state, Hold phase, strain stage and interaction targets. IK and 
additive layers follow bounded weights. Locomotion and physics state remain authoritative. Animation 
events can produce footsteps and contact sounds, but cannot be the only source of a required puzzle 
transition.
Use Cinemachine for camera blending and a custom lightweight lane/framing director for game-specific 
composition. Cinemachine’s documented camera/brain relationship supports choosing and blending virtual 
camera states; verify the selected package API before implementation. [R11]
Timeline is reserved for short coordinated events—Anja’s transfer, major reframing, station connection and 
final paired gesture. Gameplay remains active whenever safe. On skip or checkpoint restore, execute the 
same final semantic commands as a naturally completed sequence. Skipping cannot leave an actor between 
gameplay lanes or omit a critical ownership transfer.
All cinematic assets carry maximum input-lock duration and allowed skip behavior. First-time essential 
micro-events generally remain under 10–15 seconds of local alignment; repeated playback can shorten or 
skip them. The overall target is at least 95% of campaign time with ordinary movement or active station 
control available, measured in the actual build.
10.11 Environmental effects
Suspended water is a bounded authored surface/volume with a defined release animation and flow-state 
graph, not a general “freeze fluid” sandbox. Separate visual water, collision boundaries, wetness and hazard 
volumes. Final discharge follows pre-authored routes selected by the validated preparation state; its 
presentation cannot overflow into a logically safe survivor path because of a random simulation result.
Structural settling uses authored meshes, joints and debris emitters. Preserve the visible causal order: 
support receives load, main mass settles, small dust/debris follows. Rain, steam, condensation, dust and 
contact particles are pooled and culled by chunk. A frozen mechanism does not stop unrelated particles 
around it.
TAA or other anti-aliasing must be tested on thin conduits, cloth, hair and abrupt Hold starts/stops. Unity 
documents multiple URP anti-aliasing choices; use the package-compatible stock implementation before 
contemplating custom temporal rendering. Motion-vector correctness and history reset during camera 
changes are test requirements, not assumptions. [R13]

10.12 Performance budgets and profiling gates
Provisional test fixture: a six-core desktop CPU, 16 GB system RAM, a discrete GPU with 6 GB VRAM and 
SSD storage. Select actual available lab machines at M00 and record their exact models, drivers and OS 
versions. This is a test planning envelope, not a published minimum specification or purchase

Initial 1080p/60 target budgets: CPU frame work p95 ≤12 ms, GPU p95 ≤13 ms, with remaining frame

exceptions justified by profiling. Initial character LOD0 targets: Ivo 15–25K triangles; ordinary human 10–

12 — Staffing, roadmap and production gates
12.1 Staffing and schedule assumptions
The baseline assumes an experienced eight-person core: game director/level designer; technical lead; 
gameplay engineer; environment lead; environment artist; character artist/animator; technical artist; 
producer. Add contracted animation and audio support, part-time QA from preproduction and dedicated 
QA capacity during full production/beta. The director owns narrative, with an optional external narrative 
review rather than a separate full-time writing department.
This is not a realistic 24-month plan for one or two generalists at the same quality and scope. With fewer 
people, reduce unique environments/characters or extend the schedule before commissioning assets. The 
nominal plan is 96 weeks to a release candidate plus up to eight reserve/stabilization weeks. It is a planning 
model, not a date commitment. Hiring, holidays, rework, outsourcing and platform scope must be reflected 
in a funded resource plan.
Protect contingency. Estimate work in person-days after the slice establishes actual throughput; include 
integration and revision, not only model creation. A useful capacity model is available person-days × 
realistic focus allocation, compared with prioritized work packages. Do not infer budget from another 
game’s public development history. Commercial pricing, licenses, marketing and platform fees require 
project-specific quotes.
12.2 Gate definitions
Prototype proves a rule or feel. First playable proves a continuous route. Vertical slice proves the actual 
final-quality production method. End-to-end proves the entire campaign exists. Alpha is feature/content 
complete. Beta is stable and representative. Release candidate is the exact proposed shipping build. No 
milestone is passed simply because its target week arrives.
Early milestones overlap where dependencies allow. The final-quality slice is the complete 25-minute 
laundry chapter, not a disconnected montage. The first 15/30-minute builds are chronological onboarding 
tests; their art can lag the slice. This avoids confusing content coverage with quality coverage.

13 — Quality assurance, playtests and release gates
13.1 What this specification does—and does not—validate
The room cards, contracts and acceptance tests are implementation targets. No playable game, shipped 
benchmark, user study or asset pipeline has been executed as part of this document. The numerical values 
are starting constraints to test. A planning document can eliminate many contradictions; it cannot establish 
whether a jump feels right or whether an encounter is frightening without a build and fresh players.
Every milestone ends in a packaged playable build and evidence, not a presentation. A design that passes in 
the editor but fails after reload, with a different controller, or on the declared minimum hardware has not 
passed.
13.2 Test layers
Logic tests: ownership transitions, candidate filtering, support predicates, process phase capture, puzzle 
dependencies, station latches, migration and event idempotency. Run on every change affecting those 
modules. Keep ordinary data tests independent of scene rendering.
Play-mode fixtures: a compact scene for each core interaction and each puzzle grammar. Automate 
canonical input traces and assert reachable outcome, not byte-identical cosmetic physics. Every fixed 
defect receives a minimal reproduction fixture when practical.
Packaged smoke route: boot, first Hold, anchor transfer, aware enemy release, save/load, streaming seam, 
station entry/exit, final root guard, credits. Run nightly and on every candidate.
Human review: movement feel, visual affordances, horror timing, narrative inference and sound mix need 
observation. Automation cannot certify them. Use fresh players for first-read tests and returning players 
for regression and mastery tests; do not mix their timing data.
13.3 Formative playtest protocol
For early fixtures use 5–6 fresh participants per revision when feasible, observing tendencies rather than 
claiming statistical proof. The vertical slice gate uses at least 12 fresh adult players spanning familiar and 
unfamiliar puzzle-platformer experience. Beta uses approximately 24 first-time full-campaign sessions, split 
across input devices and target configurations. These are recruitment goals, not evidence already collected.
Do not coach immediately. Record what the player tries and their hypothesis. After a stuck interval, ask 
“What are you trying to make happen?” before explaining a rule. A player who understands a causal 
relationship but mistimes an action needs a different revision from one who cannot identify the interactive 
object.
At chapter 4 ask: “What happened to the worker when you used the brace?” At chapter 6 ask: “What is 
keeping this place in its current state?” After the ending ask: “What made letting go possible?” Avoid asking 
whether they noticed “conscious bearers” before they have described that idea themselves.
Record intentional ambiguity separately from failed communication. Disagreement about the technology's 
origin is acceptable. Believing that Hold rewinds injuries, that every bearer is already dead, or that the final 
flood was a random punishment is not.
13.4 Telemetry and privacy-by-design
For development builds, capture SessionStart, SequenceEnter/Exit, CheckpointSave/Load, 
HoldAcquire/Release/Transfer/Refused, StationSelect/Latch/Unlatch, PuzzleStateChange, Death, 
HintShown, Pause and PerformanceSample. Each event carries build hash, sequence ID, local session ID, 
monotonic game time, input type and relevant non-personal state IDs. No microphone recording, screen 
capture or personal information is required by gameplay telemetry.

never become the authoritative state change itself.
13.5 Priority test register
The accompanying workbook and JSON contain 48 initial test cases with setups, actions, expected results 
and first gates. They are a minimum regression foundation, not a claim that 48 cases exhaust the state 
space. Each sequence acceptance test in section 5 is additional to this register.
Critical families are movement-rate independence, carrier motion, single ownership, occupied-anchor 
refusal, no stored-energy exploit, Hold/hand availability, station semantics, AI perception honesty, save 
atomicity, active-scene pinning, restart audio, final input interruption, and complete ending continuity.
13.6 Defect severity and stop-ship policy
Critical: corrupts unrecoverable progress, prevents a campaign route, crashes predictably during required 
play, silently commits the ending on disconnect, or allows the final evacuation state to contradict the 
restored save. Stop integration or release as appropriate.
High: required puzzle sometimes becomes unsolvable; player loses progress repeatedly; input/collision 
creates unavoidable capture; low settings hide an essential clue; supported hardware cannot sustain 
agreed performance; narrative causality is systematically misunderstood by the test cohort.
Medium: recoverable animation, sound, camera or accessibility problem that harms comprehension or 
comfort but has a viable clear route. Low: cosmetic polish issue without meaningful gameplay effect. 
Severity and scheduling priority are separate fields; frequency, scope and workaround determine urgency.
At alpha, all features and content exist and every critical bug has an owner and reproduction. At beta, no 
known campaign blockers or corrupt-save faults remain, and every high issue has a closure plan before 
release. The final build has no known critical/high release blockers. A deadline does not downgrade an 
unresolved blocker.
13.7 Duration and pacing acceptance
The 225-minute allocation is a first-playthrough production target, not a required time lock. Aim for a 210–
240-minute median range in beta on the intended default experience. Report distribution, player 
familiarity, assistance and deaths; do not cherry-pick the exact 225-minute run. Skilled replays may be 
much faster; unfamiliar players may take longer.
Review any section whose median exceeds its target by more than about 30 percent, any cluster of three 
repeated deaths without a new hypothesis, and any quiet corridor where most players spend more than a 
minute with nothing meaningful to observe. These are review triggers, not automatic proof of failure. 
Preserve atmosphere where attention is active; remove space that only adds walking time.
Never lengthen the game by slowing Ivo, extending ladders, increasing enemy search duration or adding 
mandatory repeated transfers. If the full game is short, develop one under-realized spatial or narrative 
consequence already in the plan. If it is long, remove redundancy before cutting a necessary learning step.
13.8 Chapter acceptance checklist
Each chapter review must show its entry save, exit save, every failure/restart path, minimum visual setting, 
keyboard/controller run, final-quality target frame, audio event coverage, unique mechanic contribution, 
narrative inference and duration report. The director signs experience; engineering signs state integrity; 
art/audio sign readability and delivery; QA signs known-defect record; producer signs capacity and 
dependencies.

14 — Risk, capacity, scope and change control
14.1 Planning economics
This baseline assumes eight sustained core roles with specialist animation, audio and QA support, not one 
developer doing all work sequentially. The 96-week production outline plus an eight-week reserve is a 
planning scenario, not a commitment or quoted schedule. It must be recalculated using measured vertical-
slice throughput, availability, rework and funding.
Estimate cost using local actuals rather than inventing a universal indie-game price: total loaded labor by 
role and booked week, specialist contracts, software/hardware, platform/distribution services, localization, 
testing, insurance/legal support and launch assets, plus an explicit contingency. Do not hide unpaid 
overtime inside the estimate. No monetary budget is supplied here because rates, location, employment 
structure and funded team capacity are unspecified.
At the slice gate, record person-days per finished room, per hero interaction and per validated mechanism. 
Separate reusable-tool investment from repeat content cost. Forecast the remaining campaign by room 
complexity and asset novelty rather than multiplying the first polished minute blindly. Reserve rework 
capacity, particularly for animation contacts, camera composition and save-state integration.
14.2 Risk register and ownership
Fourteen initial risks are listed in the workbook, including physical instability, generic time-freeze identity, 
station complexity, animation throughput, final narrative clarity, streaming spikes, aging engine support, 
unclear rights, key-person dependency and accidental root release. Each has a named role owner, review 
gate, mitigation and scope fallback. Review high-impact items weekly until retired with evidence.
The most important early kill criterion is not visual quality. If ordinary players cannot understand personal 
versus transferred ownership, or if the mechanic produces repetitive puzzles even after two focused 
revisions, stop bulk environment production and rework the interaction model. Do not spend a year 
decorating an unproven rule.
14.3 Scope cut order
First reduce background clothing variants, unique decorative props, optional side pockets, bespoke camera 
transitions and costly secondary simulation. Next consolidate modular kits and shorten repeated traversal 
between already understood beats. Reduce the number of visible survivors before removing the 
recognizable survivor continuity.
Protect the first useful Hold, transfer revelation, aware Custodian, Orren's mechanical relief, Anja's 
irreversible integration, low-sea reveal, small station tutorial, evacuation transformation, prepared 
discharge and voluntary final hand. These are the causal spine. Removing them changes the game rather 
than merely reducing production cost.
Do not add open-world districts, combat, crafting, extensive swimming, playable flashbacks, multiplayer, 
procedural puzzles, branching morality endings or an unrestricted destruction system to this baseline. Such 
proposals require a separate funded scope decision.
14.4 Change procedure
A change request identifies the problem observed, affected sequence/system IDs, proposed alternative, 
learning consequences, asset and save-schema impact, test evidence, estimated discipline effort and 
something removed or deferred in exchange. Director, engineering lead and producer approve changes 
crossing system or chapter boundaries.
